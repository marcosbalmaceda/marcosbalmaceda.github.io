I am an incoming Assistant Professor in Entrepreneurship at Universidad del Desarrollo (UDD) in Chile, starting in January 2027. I am completing my PhD in Business Economics at KU Leuven.

My research examines how emerging technologies reshape entrepreneurship and firm organization. I focus on the information frictions and incentives they create and on the governance that responds to them, with current work centered on generative AI. With entrepreneurs, I study AI use, AI mentoring, and the role of disclosure and platform certification in raising funding. Within firms, I study technology licensing and inventor mobility, intellectual property and the organization of AI innovation, and the governance of employees’ AI use.

I work with formal models, field experiments, and large-scale observational data. In partnership with ChileConverge, I conducted a field experiment with 710 micro-business owners in Chile. With the ION Management Science Lab at HEC Paris, I co-design two randomized trials of AI mentoring, examining how it can develop skills such as personal initiative and negotiation, with one trial targeting approximately 90,000 job seekers at France Travail and another planned for 2027 targeting approximately 120,000 secondary students across Chile, Peru, and Ecuador.

My work has received the Norbert Szyperski TIM Research Award and two PhD paper prize nominations from the Strategic Management Society. I am a member of the Innovation Growth Lab research network.

Before my PhD, I was an economic adviser in the Chilean government, a think-tank researcher, and a predoctoral research assistant at the Barcelona Institute of Economics (IEB).
