I am an applied economist and incoming Assistant Professor in Entrepreneurship at Universidad del Desarrollo (UDD) in Chile, starting in January 2027. I am completing my PhD in Business Economics at KU Leuven.

My research asks how information and incentives shape what entrepreneurs and firms do with new technologies such as generative AI. With entrepreneurs, I study what holds back adoption, how platform certification affects fundraising, and how mentors and coaches, human or AI, help people absorb training in soft skills such as personal initiative and negotiation. Within firms, I study how technology licensing affects inventor mobility and how intellectual property and access policies shape AI development and use.

I use formal models, field experiments, and large-scale observational data. In partnership with ChileConverge, I ran a field experiment on profit expectations and AI use with 710 micro-business owners in Chile. With the ION Management Science Lab at HEC Paris, I co-design two randomized trials of AI mentoring and coaching. One involves about 90,000 job seekers through France Travail. The other, planned for 2027, will involve about 120,000 secondary students through Comeduc in Chile and the education ministries of Peru and Ecuador.

My research has earned the Norbert Szyperski TIM Research Award and nominations for two Strategic Management Society PhD Paper Prizes. I am a member of the Innovation Growth Lab research network.

Before my PhD, I was an economic adviser in the Chilean government, a think-tank researcher, a business intelligence engineer in the private sector, and a predoctoral research fellow at the Barcelona Institute of Economics (IEB).
